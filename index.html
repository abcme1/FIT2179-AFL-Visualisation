Where AFL Talent Comes From: The Pipeline Geography

Evaluating talent relative to population reveals a highly competitive geographical landscape. Victoria consistently anchors the league, peaking at a talent density of 0.91 drafted players per 100,000 residents in 2023. Traditional football states challenge this dominance, seen when Western Australia's density surged to 0.78 in 2022 to briefly beat Victoria's 0.77 output. Meanwhile, South Australia maintains a remarkably stable production line, consistently averaging over 0.70 per 100,000.

The data also highlights development in non-traditional markets. Large population bases keep absolute densities lower in New South Wales and Queensland, but their volume is steadily growing—evidenced by Queensland climbing from 6 players in 2020 to 11 by 2024. In contrast, the Northern Territory exposes extreme small-sample volatility, swinging from zero draftees in 2022 to a league-wide density spike of 1.13 in 2023 with just 3 selections.

Disposals by Team: Kicks vs Handballs

In Australian Rules Football (AFL), a disposal is simply any legal kick or handball that passes the ball to another player or moves it down the field. Normalizing disposal counts into a relative percentage split exposes the true tactical identity of each club, stripping away pure possession volume. The Sydney Swans stand out as the league's most extreme territory-based team, dedicating a massive 70% of their total disposals to kicking (290 kicks vs. 125 handballs). Carlton follows a closely related structural formula, keeping their kicking ratio up at 69% to favor direct, long-range ball tracking over short link-up play.

Conversely, the chart visualizes an upside-down tactical configuration for handball-heavy clubs. Hawthorn heavily leans on short, high-risk handpassing chains, with kicks making up a minor 39% share of their utility mix (162 kicks vs. 257 handballs). Collingwood displays a nearly identical handball-first signature at 39% kicks. Meanwhile, Melbourne anchors the absolute mathematical equilibrium of the league's strategic spectrum, showcasing a perfect 50% split with 195 kicks and 195 handballs.

Team Disposals vs Tackles

In the AFL, plotting total disposals against total tackles maps out a club's fundamental relationship between ball retention and defensive urgency. High-volume possession teams naturally look to control the game with the ball in hand, while high-tackle teams thrive on high-pressure, defensive work rates to win it back. The Brisbane Lions represent the ultimate possession-heavy benchmark, racking up a massive league-high of 9,750 disposals while maintaining a highly respectable 1,470 tackles. Closely following this high-octane offensive blueprint are the GWS Giants, who manage a similarly elite 9,310 disposals but layer it with a ferocious, front-half pressure game that nets them 1,670 tackles.

On the other end of the tactical spectrum sits Port Adelaide, the competition's absolute gold standard for pure defensive pressure. They lead the league with a relentless 1,680 tackles, proving they are comfortable matching their high-disposal ball movement (8,960) with punishing off-ball work. Conversely, rebuilding teams struggle to dictate terms on either front; West Coast sits at the floor of the league's ball-winning capabilities with just 7,410 disposals and 1,290 tackles, while Richmond anchors the defensive floor, recording a league-low 1,210 tackles alongside 7,650 disposals.

Crowd Trend Over the 2025 Season

In the AFL, tracking crowd trends using standard raw totals can be incredibly misleading. Because the mid-season bye rounds scale back the number of fixtures, and the elimination blocks of the postseason drop from nine matches down to just one or two, total ticket sales artificially collapse. Normalizing this narrative to look purely at the average attendance per match exposes the genuine, building fan engagement across the 2025 calendar. While the early home-and-away season maintains a healthy, consistent pulse—anchored by a massive block of 47,944 fans per game in Round 2 and peaking exceptionally in Round 13 with an average of 54,167 spectators—the mid-winter stretch experiences a predictable, cold-weather dip, bottoming out in Round 16 at 31,667 fans per match.

The true power of this metric becomes evident during the high-stakes pressure of the Finals Series. Looking strictly at aggregate numbers would show a severe drop, but the match average reveals a massive, capacity-straining surge. Crowds explode from a baseline home-and-away average up to a towering 73,750 per match in Round 25, climbing even higher to 82,500 per match in Round 27. The entire narrative culminates in the absolute mathematical pinnacle of the football year in Round 29, packing out a historic, sold-out crowd of 100,024 fans for the Grand Final.

Note: Round 28 is at 0 because it represents the AFL's official Pre-Grand Final bye week, meaning no matches are played that weekend while teams rest up for the big game.

Tracking a team's sequential form using a connected scatter plot maps the macro-narrative of a football season into clear trajectories. By plotting cumulative winning and losing margins round by round, the chart strips away single-week anomalies to reveal sustained growth, absolute stagnation, or catastrophic collapses. Carlton models the ultimate premium benchmark of steady, building dominance. Starting with an opening 22-point margin in Round 1, their line features a consistently positive slope across the entire 2025 campaign, withstanding the regular season to power all the way through the final weeks, scaling to an elite, league-high cumulative margin of 335 points by Round 29.

Meanwhile, North Melbourne represents a historical structural collapse. Club Attacking Profiles & Possessions

Contrasting absolute scoring volume against weekly possession metrics exposes the clear relationship between field execution styles and offensive efficiency. By pairing total season goals with average match disposals on a radial axis, the data isolates which teams run highly clinical, high-yield forward entries from those trapped in high-possession over-passing chains. The Sydney Swans claim the absolute attacking mantle of the league, pairing a lethal competition-high of 362 total goals with a dominant 388 average match disposals. Carlton tracks a closely related aggressive blueprint, striking an elite balance with 355 season goals from a heavy 382 disposal engine to lead the frontline competition.

Conversely, the radial distribution uncovers deep inefficiencies and stark scoring ceilings across the lower tiers of the ladder. Essendon exposes a prominent over-passing signature, burning a high volume of ball movement with 372 average match disposals but failing to connect inside 50, resulting in a low tally of just 275 season goals. Meanwhile, rebuilding clubs face a bruising battle on both fronts; Richmond and West Coast struggle to construct effective ball movement or find functional avenues to goal, anchoring the base of the charts with just 230 goals (from 342 disposals) and a league-low 222 goals (from 338 disposals) respectively.
